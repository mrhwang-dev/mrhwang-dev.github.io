Top story · Campus Press Hub · Live

850,000 scattered campus stories,
found in one place

I planned and run a search service that gathers stories from 432 student outlets at 195 Korean universities and links readers to the originals.

SEOUL — Stories from Korea's student newspapers, campus broadcasters and campus magazines sit on a different website for every school. To see how reporters at another school covered the same issue, you had to open their sites one by one. Scattered like that, they are a chore to find, and even good reporting goes unread.

Campus Press Hub puts those stories in one place. Readers can filter by section, outlet type, date and format, and follow the universities they care about.

It does not republish full articles. Readers search on the hub and read on the original outlet's site. The hub publishes its collection policy and a transparency report, and gives outlets a way to request corrections or removal.

About 1,700 copies vanished within three hours of distribution.

In 2015, as a first-year, I spent two months reporting on a college student-council president improperly collecting a scholarship and on alleged embezzlement of the student club federation's funds, and the story ran on Jodae Sinmun's front page. Within three hours of distribution the papers were gone, and the police opened an investigation. I was dismissed from the paper over the story. In 2020, at the suggestion of the paper's new faculty adviser, I came back as a correspondent and spent three months reporting the election series with a junior reporter.

In the award interview I said student journalists outside Seoul needed a way to meet senior journalists, and laid out the idea of a network and an academy. That same year I became chair of the University Journalists Network (2021–2022) and opened the academy.
