Working rules for an editor who can't read code

By Chiwoong Hwang · First draft by Claude · November 20, 2026

I can't read code, so reading it won't tell me whether what the AI built is right. Instead I work the way I learned at the student paper. Just as I checked the facts before a story ran, I use every screen myself before it goes live.

One: split the work, and set the rules myself. Claude builds; agy (Antigravity) edits the copy. They work in the same repository but may touch different files. agy edits only the manuscript and may not change numbers or dates. I wrote these rules into the repository's instructions.

Two: if it's unknown, leave it blank. AI tends to fill a gap with a plausible sentence. I have it leave square brackets wherever my own experience belongs, and mark unclear fixes "on hold" instead of guessing.

Three: I click through the results myself. When I clicked the link to a UNN (University News Network) story that I had first put in this portfolio, the page said the story didn't exist. I replaced it with a Hankook Ilbo story on the same events.

Four: strike errors through, don't erase them. The automated check that ran 248 times in two days and used up a month's allowance, and the database bill that came in $33 over, both still sit in the corrections at the end of the story.

This piece was written the same way. Claude wrote the first draft, and I decided what to change.
